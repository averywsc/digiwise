Real or scam

Ten messages. Some are fake. Spot which ones before you tap.

What scammers actually want

Scammers rarely out-hack anyone. They want you to act on a feeling before you think. Most of the time they’re after one of these.

  1. Your moneyFake fees, fake sellers and requests for gift cards.
  2. Your loginsA fake sign-in page that copies your password and your code.
  3. Your accountsOnce they’re in, they message your friends as you and the scam spreads.
  4. Your personal infoYour name, address, ID and photos can be used or sold.

Anyone can get caught. Being rushed, tired or scrolling fast is when it happens.

The five tells

  1. PressureYou have 24 hours, act now, last chance. Real organisations give you time and more than one way to sort things out.
  2. A strange linkRead the address slowly. Look for a zero in place of an o, extra words, dashes and odd endings.
  3. It asks for somethingPasswords, one-time codes, card details, or payment before you get anything. No real service needs your code.
  4. Too good or too scaryFree Nitro, a surprise refund, an account about to be deleted. Both pull on your emotions.
  5. It doesn’t sound like themA mate who never sends links suddenly does. A company says Dear user instead of your name.

One tell is a warning. Two or more is almost always a scam.

Check before you tap

  1. StopNothing real is so urgent it can’t wait two minutes.
  2. Look at who sent itCheck the name, the number and the address, not just the logo.
  3. Don’t tap the linkOpen the real app yourself or type the site into your browser.
  4. AskMessage the person on another app, or ask a parent, teacher or friend.
  5. Search itSearch the message online. Scams get reported fast and others will have seen it.
Address check. A real address ends right after the name, like nzpost.co.nz. A fake adds extra words and dashes, like nzpost-redeliver.co.

If you already tapped

It happens to smart people. What matters is what you do next.

  1. Stop and don’t replyDon’t tap anything else or enter more details.
  2. Lock it downChange the password on that account and anywhere you reuse it. Turn on two-factor sign-in and log out other devices.
  3. Money or card details wentContact your bank straight away.
  4. Tell someoneA parent or teacher can help quickly, and nobody will think you’re dumb.
  5. Report itForward scam texts free to 7726 and report scams to Netsafe at netsafe.org.nz.

Three things that beat most scams.

  1. Urgency plus a link is the classic combo. Slow down.
  2. Don’t tap the link. Open the real app or site yourself.
  3. If a friend sends something odd, message them somewhere else to check it was really them.
